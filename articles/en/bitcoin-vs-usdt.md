# Bitcoin or USDT: which to choose

The two most common tools when exchanging are **Bitcoin (BTC)** and **USDT (Tether)**. They solve different
tasks, so "which is better" depends on the goal: moving money, preserving value, or entering an asset with
growth potential.

## The key difference — volatility

- **USDT** is a [stablecoin](/en/blog/slovar-terminov-obmena/) pegged to the dollar (1 USDT ≈ 1 USD). Its
  value barely changes — handy for settlements and holding "in dollars".
- **Bitcoin** has a free rate that can rise and fall by tens of percent. It's an asset, not a "digital dollar".

## For transfers

To move an amount quickly without rate risk people more often take **USDT** on the cheap
[TRC20](/en/blog/usdt-seti-trc20-erc20-bep20/) network: a low [network fee](/en/blog/komissii-setey-tron-eth-bsc/)
and a predictable amount received. Bitcoin is pricier and slower for transfers, and the rate can move during
the transfer.

## For holding

- Want to lock value in dollars — **USDT**.
- Ready for swings for the sake of growth potential — **Bitcoin**.

## Fees and networks

- BTC — a Bitcoin network fee that depends on load; there is [Bitcoin LN](/en/valuta/bitcoin-ln/) for small fast payments.
- USDT — the fee depends on the [network](/en/blog/usdt-seti-trc20-erc20-bep20/): TRC20/BEP20 are cheaper, ERC20 is pricier.

## In short

- USDT — stability and cheap transfers; Bitcoin — an asset with a moving rate.
- For a rate-risk-free transfer — usually [USDT TRC20](/en/valuta/tether-trc20/).
- For a bet on growth — [Bitcoin](/en/valuta/bitcoin/).
